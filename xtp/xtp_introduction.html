1. Introduction¶

VOTCA-XTP calculates electronic excitations of molecules and molecular materials and the transport of charges and excitons between them:

  • Electronic structure of single molecules and clusters: density-functional theory (the internal DFT engine or ORCA), quasiparticle energies in the GW approximation and neutral excitations from the Bethe-Salpeter equation (GW-BSE), see Electronic Structure Theories, Implementation Notes and GW-BSE in Practice: Methods, Numerics and Settings.

  • Excitations in a molecular environment: quantum-classical (QM/MM) embedding with polarizable classical regions (Thole or charge-response models, cut-off or Ewald summation) and embedded GW-BSE, see Polarizable Ewald Embedding, Polarizable Environments: Thole and Charge-Response Models and Embedded GW-BSE: Screening by a Polarizable Environment.

  • Charge and exciton transport in morphologies from molecular dynamics: site energies and electronic couplings for all neighbouring molecules, Marcus rates, kinetic Monte Carlo, time-of-flight and graph random walks, and stochastic network models for large systems, see Charge and Energy Transfer Theory, Charge and Energy Transport Model and Transport Simulations.

The programs are

  • xtp_tools: calculations that do not need a morphology (e.g. dftgwbse for a single molecule),

  • xtp_map: maps an MD trajectory to a state file (state.hdf5) with the molecules (“segments”) of the morphology,

  • xtp_run: calculators that act on the whole state file (neighbour list, analysis, KMC and other transport calculations),

  • xtp_parallel: job-based calculators that run many independent quantum-chemical or QM/MM calculations on the state file.

Their options are listed in Workflow and Calculator Structure; -d <name> prints the options of a calculator and --cite <name> the papers to cite for it. A Python (ASE) interface is described in XTP ASE Interface. The tutorials show complete workflows.
