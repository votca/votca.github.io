3. Projection-Based Embedding (DFT-in-DFT)¶

Many systems of interest are too large for a GW-BSE calculation of the whole, while the excitation of interest is confined to a part of it: a chromophore with long side chains, a molecule at a donor-acceptor interface, a dye in its solvation shell. Projection-based embedding [Manby:2012] splits such a system into an active region A, which keeps its full electronic structure, and an inactive region B, which is frozen at the DFT level. At the DFT level the split is exact: with the same functional in both regions, the embedded calculation reproduces the total energy and density of the full system. GW-BSE then works with the orbitals of the active region only, which reduces the size of the three-centre integrals and of the BSE Hamiltonian [Sundaram:2024].

3.1. Theory¶

The starting point is a Kohn-Sham calculation of the full system with density matrix \(\gamma\). Its occupied orbitals are localized with the Pipek-Mezey scheme [Pipek:1989]. A localized orbital belongs to the active region if its Mulliken population on at least one of the active atoms exceeds a threshold (0.4 by default); all others belong to B. This splits the density,

\[\gamma = \gamma_A^0 + \gamma_B .\]

The active region is then optimized in the field of the frozen inactive density. With \(g[\gamma]\) the Coulomb and exchange-correlation potential of a density and the embedding potential

\[v_\text{emb} = g[\gamma_A^0 + \gamma_B] - g[\gamma_A^0] ,\]

the active electrons see the Fock matrix

\[F_A = h + g[\gamma_A] + v_\text{emb} + \mu P_B , \qquad P_B = S \gamma_B S ,\]

and the total energy is

\[E[\gamma_A] = E_\text{DFT}[\gamma_A] + E_\text{DFT}[\gamma] - E_\text{DFT}[\gamma_A^0] + \mathrm{tr}\left[(\gamma_A - \gamma_A^0)\, v_\text{emb}\right] + \mu\, \mathrm{tr}\left[\gamma_A P_B\right] .\]

The projector \(P_B\) with a large level shift \(\mu\) keeps the active orbitals orthogonal to the inactive ones: in the embedded calculation the inactive orbitals appear as levels near \(\mu\), far above all physical levels, and the active electrons cannot fall into them. At self-consistency \(\gamma_A = \gamma_A^0\), and the energy equals the full one; the log reports the difference (typically \(10^{-7}\) Ha).

The embedded calculation can also run in a basis restricted to the active atoms and the border atoms of the inactive region, i.e. those with a basis function whose Mulliken population in \(\gamma_A^0\) exceeds truncation_threshold [Barnes:2013], [Bennie:2015]. This makes the embedded calculation itself cheaper, but the result then depends on the threshold; [Sundaram:2024] found it necessary to tighten it from \(10^{-4}\) to \(10^{-5}\) for a charge-transfer complex.

3.2. Running an embedded calculation¶

In xtp_tools -e dftgwbse the embedding needs the tasks localize and dft_in_dft after the DFT calculation of the full system, and the active atoms in dftpackage.xtpdft.dft_in_dft:

<dftgwbse>
  <job_name>system</job_name>
  <dftpackage>
    <basisset>def2-svp</basisset>
    <auxbasisset>aux-def2-svp</auxbasisset>
    <functional>XC_HYB_GGA_XC_PBEH</functional>
    <xtpdft>
      <dft_in_dft>
        <activeatoms>0:15 105 106</activeatoms>
        <threshold>0.4</threshold>
        <levelshift>100000</levelshift>
        <truncate_basis>false</truncate_basis>
      </dft_in_dft>
    </xtpdft>
  </dftpackage>
  <tasks>input,dft,parse,localize,dft_in_dft,gwbse</tasks>
  <localize>
    <method>jacobi-sweeps</method>
  </localize>
  <gwbse>
    ...
  </gwbse>
</dftgwbse>

The atom indices refer to the order of the atoms in the input geometry and use the range syntax a:b (inclusive) with single indices, separated by spaces or commas.

option (dft_in_dft)

meaning

activeatoms

atoms of the active region

threshold

Mulliken population on an active atom above which a localized orbital is active (default 0.4)

levelshift

\(\mu\) in Hartree (default \(10^4\))

truncate_basis, truncation_threshold

restrict the basis to active and border atoms (default off, \(10^{-4}\))

grow, grow_window, grow_threshold

grow the active region from the frontier orbitals (see below; default off, 0.1 eV, 0.0025)

The localization (localize.method) is done with a unitary optimizer by default; jacobi-sweeps can be more robust for large molecules.

In a qmmm job the same dft_in_dft block goes into the dftpackage of the QM region, together with a localize block; the active atoms are then numbered in the order of the atoms of the QM region. The classical regions see the full density of the QM region.

3.3. Choosing the active region¶

The active region is a modelling choice, and the results of an embedded GW-BSE calculation depend on it more than on anything else. Two things should hold:

  • the orbitals that take part in the excitation lie in it: an exciton whose hole extends across the cut cannot be described by the active orbitals;

  • the cut goes through bonds that are electronically unimportant, such as the single bond to a saturated side chain, and not through a conjugated system.

To help with this, the embedding step reports, before it starts:

  • whether the active atoms of each molecule form one connected piece of the bond graph (a region that splits a molecule stops the run);

  • the coverage of the frontier orbitals of the full calculation: the fraction of each occupied orbital within grow_window of the HOMO (Mulliken population) that lies on the active region, and the inactive atoms on which they have weight;

  • inactive hydrogens bonded to active atoms and inactive heavy atoms bonded to two or more active ones.

With grow the region is extended automatically: every atom of the molecules in the active region whose population in one of these frontier orbitals exceeds grow_threshold is added, together with the shortest chain of bonds that connects it to the region, then heavy atoms bonded to two or more active atoms, and the hydrogens of all active atoms. Without a truncated basis only the occupied frontier orbitals matter, since the embedded calculation keeps all virtual orbitals; with truncate_basis the virtual frontier orbitals are included as well.

3.4. GW-BSE in the active region¶

GW-BSE uses the occupied orbitals of the active region and, without a truncated basis, all virtual orbitals of the full basis; the inactive levels shifted to \(\mu\) are left out. The QP and BSE windows refer to these orbitals. A frozen core (ignore_corelevels) counts only the core levels of the active atoms.

Two effects of the inactive region are missing in this plain form: it does not screen the interaction (the QP gap comes out too large and the electron-hole attraction too strong; the two errors partly cancel in the excitation energies), and its occupied orbitals are missing from the Green’s function. Both can be added with gwbse.inactive_screening and gwbse.inactive_screening_complete_g, described in Projection-based embedding: screening by the inactive region. The active region can be embedded in a polarizable environment at the same time (Embedded GW-BSE: Screening by a Polarizable Environment).

The embedded GW-BSE is implemented for closed-shell active regions with an even number of electrons.
